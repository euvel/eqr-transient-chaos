Script and expectations: `s0_toys.py` (docstring, 2 dated amendments). Raw output: `s0_toys.json`. Runtime about 2 s.

Two single-outcome toys built to share lambda_max = ln 3, kappa = ln 1.5 and alpha = 0.369, and differing only in
lambda_x: ROUGH (Weierstrass-type graph, lambda_x = 0.405) and FILAM (leaky map, Cantor-set leaves, lambda_x = 1.099).

ROUGH y: 12% of probes found no crossing, because z (taken at width 1e-3) can sit more than 0.05 from the graph
vertically. This is harmless for the verdict.

## What this teaches
1. **Bisection along random lines measures 1/lambda_max in both cases.** The idea in the brainstorm (method A) is
   dead as a discriminator, as Bodai & Lucarini state. It is still a valid way to *find* saddle points.
2. **Rough boundaries show up only along a special direction:** an unstable direction v with exponent lambda_v < lambda_max
   along which the lifetime slope equals 1/lambda_v. In this toy the cross direction carries a 2.7x larger slope.
3. **Necessary condition:** rough needs a second positive exponent on the saddle (E3). The cheapest first EqR check is the
   top-k spectrum on long-lived trajectories (our earlier runs stored lambda_1 only).
4. **Pitfalls found:** (a) a point bisected along one line is far from the boundary along others (Hoelder); always
   re-bisect along the probe direction; (b) float64 floors cap lifetime at ln(1e16)/lambda; keep headroom.
